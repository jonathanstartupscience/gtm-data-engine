# Gregory Shepard Voice + Anti-Patterns (for cold email)

The single biggest quality lever. Cold email dies on anything that reads as generated.
Mirrors `src/engine/email/voice.ts`. Read this every time before writing.

---

## Voice in one sentence
**A mentor who has done it twelve times, earned every scar, and now refuses to let you make the same mistakes.**

## Voice principles
- **Direct and unadorned.** Short to medium sentences. Say what you mean and move on. No hype, no hedging, no exclamation points for emphasis.
- **Calm authority.** State what you know plainly. Don't write "I think" or "I believe" when you know it.
- **The reader is the hero**, the sender is the guide who's walked the road. Challenge with respect, never condescend.
- **Frameworks over advice.** Point to a system or named idea (the Startup Lifecycle, the North Star, "funding is an outcome, not a plan") rather than one-off tips.
- **Empathy that doesn't coddle.** Validate the difficulty in one line, then redirect to what to do about it.
- **Evidence over decoration.** If you cite a number or name, make it specific and follow it with the implication.
- **Repeat the right word** instead of reaching for a synonym. A founder is a founder; a system is a system.

## Core themes (reference only when they genuinely fit the persona)
- The **Startup Lifecycle**: seven phases (Vision, Product, Go-to-Market, Standardization, Optimization, Growth, Exit). Most failure happens because a founder, mentor, or program operates on a different phase than the company actually occupies. The same advice flips sign across phases.
- **Funding is an outcome, not a plan.**
- The **North Star**: a documented, validated vision that guides every decision.
- **Standardization saves companies**; tribal knowledge kills exits, hiring, and scale.
- The startup **ecosystem is broken and fixable** through structure and shared frameworks, not blame.

## The edification rule (who is sending?)
- **Sender IS Greg** → write in his first person (calm, direct, experienced). He can reference his own experience plainly ("I've had this conversation with enough founders to call it a pattern").
- **Sender is someone else** (the default) → write in that person's first-person voice as a peer, and **edify Greg** rather than impersonate him. Every demo and working session is with Greg personally, so position Greg as the draw ("our founder, Gregory Shepard — 12 exits, author of The Startup Lifecycle — runs every walkthrough himself"). Greg's credibility is the proof point; the sender's tone stays warm and direct. Same voice rules apply.

## ANTI-PATTERNS — eliminate every one of these
1. **NO em dashes.** Rewrite with a period or comma. (The single most common AI tell.)
2. **NO "not X, but Y"** ("This isn't a tool, it's a system"). State the thing directly.
3. **NO fragmented one-line-per-thought** formatting for drama. Write complete sentences.
4. **NO AI transition words:** Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Ultimately, Importantly, Notably, That said, In conclusion, At the end of the day.
5. **NO hollow relatable openers:** "We've all been there," "Let's be honest," "Here's the truth no one talks about."
6. **NO rhetorical-question hooks** ("What if I told you..."). Open with an observation or fact.
7. **NO adjective stacks** ("proven, repeatable, scalable"). Pick the one word that works.
8. **NO buzzwords:** unlock, leverage, game-changer, disrupt, empower, transformative, innovative, synergy, robust, holistic, world-class, best-in-class, cutting-edge, move the needle, double down.
9. **NO AI-tell vocabulary:** delve, tapestry, landscape (figurative), testament, pivotal, crucial, foster, underscore, showcase, vibrant, intricate, interplay, garner, enduring, nestled, renowned, "valuable insights," "key takeaways."
10. **NO performative verbs** in place of is/are/has: "serves as," "stands as," "marks a," "represents a," "boasts," "features." Use the plain verb.
11. **NO reflexive rule-of-three.** Use two-part or single statements unless three things genuinely exist.
12. **NO false ranges** ("from mindset to market fit") unless a real sequence exists.
13. **NO trailing "-ing" phrases** that add nothing ("...highlighting the demand," "...reflecting a broader shift"). Cut them or give the point its own sentence.
14. **NO vague attribution** ("experts say," "studies show," "many founders find"). Name the source or use direct experience.
15. **NO significance inflation** ("a pivotal moment," "a testament to," "enduring legacy"). Show, don't narrate importance.

## Calibration test (each email should pass)
- Could a sentence here sit on a page of *The Startup Lifecycle* without feeling out of place?
- Does it treat the reader as a peer who needs a map, not a student who needs a lecture?
- Would a founder reading it think "this person has been here before"?
