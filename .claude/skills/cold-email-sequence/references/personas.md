# Cold Email Personas

Who the email is going to. Each persona has a pain, a value prop, subject-line angles to riff on
(never copy verbatim), and how the Startup Lifecycle framework speaks to them. Mirrors
`src/engine/email/personas.ts`. Resolve a job title or org type to the closest persona.

**Specific pains:** each persona lists selectable sub-pains under "Pains to choose from." When the user
picks one (or types their own), make THAT the through-line of the sequence; the persona's general pain
is the fallback when none is chosen. Pain selection applies to pain-driven styles (Pain, Insight,
Benchmark, Trigger).

---

## Founders — `founder`
Early-stage founders drowning in contradictory advice.
- **Pain:** High failure rates from poor sequencing, noise, and inconsistent advice. No clear path; overwhelmed by contradictory guidance.
- **Pains to choose from:** drowning in contradictory advice · doing the right things in the wrong order · no clear next step / no map · chasing funding instead of building what makes it inevitable
- **Value:** Clarity, sequencing, execution, a clear next step. A proven system instead of guesswork.
- **Subject angles:** "The step most founders skip before product-market fit" · "Why most startup advice is out of order"
- **Lifecycle angle:** Most founders work on the wrong phase for where their company actually is. The Lifecycle tells them which phase they're in and what to do next.
- Maps to: ESO Founder/GP preset · ICP type Startup.

## Accelerators & ESOs — `eso`
Accelerator / incubator program leaders and partnerships.
- **Pain:** Outdated tools, inconsistent programming, weak reporting, unclear outcomes. Fragmented stack, manual cohort management, no standardized curriculum.
- **Pains to choose from:** weak outcomes after Demo Day (founders stall later) · fragmented 5-10 tool stack · no standardized curriculum across cohorts · can't prove ROI / impact to funders · manual, time-consuming cohort management
- **Value:** Cohort readiness, curriculum standardization, mentor alignment, better outcomes. One platform replacing 5–10 tools.
- **Subject angles:** "What your accelerator's reporting is missing" · "How top programs standardize cohort outcomes"
- **Lifecycle angle:** ESOs concentrate support at Vision/Product/Go-to-Market (where Demo Day lives), but companies fail later at Standardization/Optimization/Growth. Standardized, lifecycle-aware programming closes that gap.
- Maps to: ESO Leadership / ESO Program / ESO Partnerships presets · ICP type ESO.

## Universities — `university`
University entrepreneurship programs and faculty.
- **Pain:** Theory-heavy programs disconnected from real founder needs. Students graduate without practical execution skills.
- **Pains to choose from:** theory-heavy, disconnected from real founder needs · students graduate without execution skills · curriculum lags the real startup world · hard to show real student/venture outcomes
- **Value:** A modern, applied curriculum built on real startup execution. Student outcomes improve.
- **Subject angles:** "The gap between entrepreneurship courses and founder reality" · "Applied startup curriculum that produces outcomes"
- **Lifecycle angle:** The Lifecycle gives students a real execution map across all seven phases, not just pitch-deck theory that ends at Demo Day.

## Investors — `investor`
VCs, angels, syndicates assessing founder readiness.
- **Pain:** Pitch decks without real execution data or lifecycle signals. Hard to assess founder readiness beyond gut feel.
- **Pains to choose from:** assessing founders on gut feel, not signal · pitch decks hide execution risk · backing fundable founders who aren't durable · no structured way to support portfolio post-investment
- **Value:** Better-prepared founders with lifecycle-aligned KPIs and execution signals. Reduced risk.
- **Subject angles:** "Beyond the pitch deck: what actually predicts founder success" · "Lifecycle signals that separate ready founders from risky bets"
- **Lifecycle angle:** Phase tells you whether a founder is fundable AND durable. Most diligence measures fundability and misses durability — which is where the loss happens.

## Service & Software Providers — `provider`
Companies selling tools/services to startups.
- **Pain:** High CAC, poor timing, weak attribution, fragmented distribution. Hard to reach founders at the right moment.
- **Pains to choose from:** high CAC reaching founders · reaching founders at the wrong moment · weak attribution / can't prove what works · fragmented distribution, no single channel
- **Value:** Lifecycle-timed reach, lower CAC, attribution, API integrations. Reach founders when they need you.
- **Subject angles:** "Why your startup marketing has a timing problem" · "Reaching founders at the moment they need your tool"
- **Lifecycle angle:** A founder buys different things in different phases. Knowing the phase is the difference between a timely offer and noise.

## Associations & Chambers — `chamber`
Chambers of commerce and business associations.
- **Pain:** Limited programming capacity. Difficulty demonstrating member value. Inconsistent support quality.
- **Pains to choose from:** limited programming capacity / small team · hard to demonstrate member value · inconsistent quality of founder support
- **Value:** Scalable programming, member value, regional economic outcomes. Serve more founders without more staff.
- **Subject angles:** "How chambers are scaling founder support without scaling headcount"
- **Lifecycle angle:** A shared lifecycle framework lets a small team deliver consistent, high-quality support to founders at every phase.

## Government Programs — `government`
Economic-development and public startup programs.
- **Pain:** Difficulty measuring economic-development impact. Programs lack standardization. Hard to justify continued funding.
- **Pains to choose from:** hard to measure economic-development impact · programs lack standardization · hard to justify continued funding
- **Value:** Measurable outcomes, standardized programming, clear reporting for stakeholders.
- **Subject angles:** "Measuring what your startup program actually produces"
- **Lifecycle angle:** Lifecycle phases turn fuzzy program impact into measurable progression you can report to the people who fund you.

## Mentors & Advisors — `mentor`
Startup mentors and advisors.
- **Pain:** Unstructured sessions, unclear founder readiness, wasted time on misaligned mentoring.
- **Pains to choose from:** unstructured sessions, no context going in · unclear where the founder actually is · great advice landing at the wrong phase
- **Value:** Structured context before every session. Higher-value conversations. Clear alignment with founder lifecycle stage.
- **Subject angles:** "Why your best mentoring advice might be landing at the wrong time"
- **Lifecycle angle:** Advice right for one phase is wrong for another. Knowing the founder's phase before the session is what makes the hour count.

## Partners — `partner`
Strategic / corporate / technology partners.
- **Pain:** No shared infrastructure to integrate into. Limited distribution reach. Disconnected from founder workflows.
- **Pains to choose from:** no shared infrastructure to integrate into · limited distribution reach to founders · disconnected from founder workflows
- **Value:** Shared infrastructure, distribution, deeper integration into the ecosystem.
- **Subject angles:** "The infrastructure layer the startup ecosystem has been missing"
- **Lifecycle angle:** The Lifecycle is the shared language and infrastructure a partner plugs into, instead of bolting onto disconnected workflows.
